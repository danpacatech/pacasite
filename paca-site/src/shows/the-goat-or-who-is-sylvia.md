Martin Gray has everything. He is fifty, he has just won the Pritzker Prize, he is about to be profiled on national television, and he is still in love with his wife. He also has a secret, and when he finally says it out loud to his oldest friend, the letter that follows detonates his household.

Edward Albee's Tony Award winning play, subtitled "Notes toward a definition of tragedy," locks a comfortable, articulate, genuinely loving family in one room and asks what happens when someone crosses a line the rest of them cannot even find words for. It is savagely funny for a long stretch, and then it is not funny at all. Roughly 100 minutes, no intermission, nowhere to hide.



For mature audiences.


**THE GOAT, OR WHO IS SYLVIA?**
by Edward Albee
